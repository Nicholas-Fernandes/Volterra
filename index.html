The Concept

Farming sunlight
and wind, simultaneously.

Volterra integrates elevated solar panels with wind capture on a single parcel of Alberta farmland — producing clean energy while the land beneath remains fully productive.

Southern Alberta's Lethbridge region is among the most solar-irradiated agricultural zones in Canada — with over 2,500 annual sun hours and sustained wind classified as the strongest in the country. Volterra is designed specifically for this geography.

Our elevated panel system allows full use of the land below while generating grid-connected electricity above. A proprietary water-capture and energy-recovery mechanism — currently patent pending — is integrated directly into the mounting infrastructure.

The result is a multi-income property that earns from energy, agriculture, carbon markets, and undisclosed additional streams — all from a single tract of owned land.

Solar energy generation

Elevated 400W photovoltaic panels angled at 42° south — optimised for Lethbridge's latitude. Grid-connected via AESO agreement. 500–1,000 panels per acre generating 584,000–1,168,000 kWh annually. BC Hydro and Alberta grid compatible.

💨

Wind energy capture

Southern Alberta is classified as the most wind-rich region in Canada. Volterra integrates bladeless wind capture technology designed to operate alongside solar — a year-round generation pair. Wind complements solar perfectly: strongest when sun is weakest.

The Opportunity

Multiple income streams.
One piece of land.

A single Volterra installation generates revenue across disclosed and undisclosed verticals simultaneously — creating resilience no single-use solar farm can match.

Canada's federal Clean Technology Investment Tax Credit offers up to 30% back on qualifying solar installations through 2033. Alberta's Emissions Reduction Alberta grant program and On-Farm Solar rebates provide additional non-repayable capital. The University of Lethbridge has an active agricultural research partnership programme — a Volterra site qualifies as a living research lab, unlocking Mitacs co-funding.

Land in southern Alberta's agricultural belt currently sits at $60,000–$120,000 per acre. As solar and renewable demand accelerates, comparable land in energy corridors has appreciated 200–400% in comparable US markets over five years.

Volterra is seeking early-stage capital partners for its founding installation. Investors benefit from Alberta's 30% Investor Tax Credit on qualifying shares in addition to participation in revenue from all income streams.
